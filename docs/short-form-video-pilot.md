# Earth to Ear — Short-Form Video Pilot

**Standing pre-podcast production pathway · November 11, 2026**  
**Status:** Video strategy approved as a direction to prototype. No accounts opened, videos filmed, clips licensed or public posts made yet.

## Correcting the launch plan

Earth to Ear is a **music listening experience**. A social strategy built primarily around text quotes, static carousels or eventual podcasts lacks a credible entry point for people who discover music through moving images and sound. **Short-form video is a foundational content format from the outset, not a by-product of a future podcast.**

Primary outlets: **Instagram Reels + YouTube Shorts simultaneously**, using a shared *original* production workflow. Bluesky supports discussion around released videos, research and listening questions. X remains optional.

A video must earn the visitor's pause with either musical audio we are actually permitted to use or an intrinsically engaging demonstration, observation or story. Avoid talking-head content that merely announces the project. A sound-led video is especially valuable, but unauthorized music is not a viable shortcut.

## Repeatable video series

| Working format | Usual length | What happens on screen and in sound | Destination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Listening Invitation | 20–40s | A **rights-cleared** musical excerpt or original commissioned demonstration is the hook; minimal labels identify performer and place; one invitation: “What did you notice?” | A specific recording in Earth to Ear's library |
| Then → Between → Now | 45–75s | A focused thread within one style and one place, across **at least three** consequential performers or moments, not two famous endpoints; original narration, properly attributed images, legally cleared musical samples where available | An editorial musical journey |
| What Did You Hear? | 30–60s | Founder or guest on camera describing *a personal listening reaction*: what surprised them, what the music brought to mind, or what they want to replay; avoid presenting personal associations as musical history | Relevant original recording and optional journal |
| Meet the Musician / Sound | 45–90s | A musician or maker with consent demonstrates a sonic technique in their own recording, or an original field/recording process with permitted audio | Source artist, recording or interview page |
| One Sound, One Question | 15–30s | A sonically engaging **original** rhythm or instrument sound with a plain listener-centered question; an abstract motion visual can serve as supporting imagery, never as a substitute for audio | About / How to Listen |

Formats should be modular. A 60-second story can later become one paragraph in a listening article, a playlist note and a prompt in the private founder research dashboard.

## A three-video *pre-launch* pilot

### V-001 — “The first ten seconds can change what you hear”

**Purpose:** Introduce the approach without requiring a famous song.  
**Visual:** Original close-up of a person listening, hand tapping, or founder speaking briefly; Earth to Ear masthead only at end.  
**Audio:** Original voice and *owned or properly licensed* subtle soundscape; let silence create emphasis if useful.  
**Script sketch:**
- 0–3s: “What do you notice before you decide whether you like a song?”
- 3–15s: Show a listener noticing rhythm, voice or timbre; use actual audible original/cleared sound if available.
- 15–25s: “Sometimes a recording takes us somewhere. Sometimes we simply want to hear it again.”
- 25–30s: “Earth to Ear. Pick a recording. See where it takes you.”

### V-002 — “The rhythm behind a reggae recording”

**Purpose:** A specific Jamaican roots-reggae listening invitation, not a history of all reggae.  
**Visual:** Original motion graphics illustrating bass and drum interplay; original musician demonstration filmed with consent if possible.  
**Audio:** A **demonstrator's own** rhythm performed and recorded for the project, or a commercially/online-use-cleared excerpt from an identifiable roots recording. Do not describe a recreated beat as an archival or actual artist recording.  
**Script sketch:** “Listen for the space between the bass and drums. How does that space change the way the song moves? In Earth to Ear, we follow that sound across recordings and generations.”  
**Call to action:** Link to the relevant *real* source recording and, when published, the narrow reggae journey.

### V-003 — “Different decades, same question”

**Purpose:** Seed the jazz vocal evolution series without misrepresenting a linear lineage.  
**Visual:** Three documented historical time points, with rights-cleared photos/art and captions for intervening performers; founder voiceover.  
**Audio:** Primarily original narration; actual clips only if the specific *recordings* are cleared. Otherwise do not imply the listener has heard the music in the post.  
**Script sketch:** “A century of jazz singing isn't a straight line between two famous voices. Listen to what happens when singers reshape the melody, delay a phrase or change the emotional weight of a familiar lyric.”  
**CTA:** A musical journey once ready; in the meantime link to an existing relevant recording or the public library.

## Minimum viable workflow

1. Pick **one already cataloged recording or one tightly defined journey question**. Write a three-sentence spoken hook, one thing to notice, and one invitation to listen.
2. Establish **exactly what sound the video may legally contain** before filming. Seek explicit consent from a performer for their newly created demonstration, use wholly owned original audio, or verify rights to both composition and sound recording for the desired online promotional use. Appropriately licensed CC recordings can qualify when license covers redistribution/derivative use, commercial use when applicable, credit and any share-alike terms. Archival availability or a YouTube link does **not** constitute permission.
3. Film original **vertical 9:16**, target **1080×1920**, using a smartphone; a quiet room, stable framing and clear voice are more important initially than expensive cameras. Optional simple lavalier microphone. An on-camera founder is welcome but not required for every video.
4. Add **large, accessible captions**; keep text clear of Instagram/YouTube overlays. Use original artwork, properly licensed or public-domain images, and correctly credited photos.
5. Edit one **platform-neutral clean master** containing only owned or cross-platform-cleared assets. Export variants per platform only if using platform-specific in-app audio. Never move an Instagram-licensed track into a YouTube upload, or vice versa, based only on its availability in one editor.
6. Publish **one to three intentional pilot videos** before committing to cadence. Link them to the correct Earth to Ear listening destination, not automatically the homepage.
7. Review 3 practical measures: **view retention/completion**, meaningful comments/saves, and actual relevant site visits. Watch what invites more listening, not only what maximizes scrolling.

## Rights: no back door

- The *original source link* used by Earth to Ear's catalog does **not** authorize extraction of audio or footage for a promotional Reel/Short.
- "A short clip" is not automatically fair use or public domain. A public-domain composition may have a copyrighted modern sound recording and arrangement.
- **Meta Sound Collection** and **YouTube Audio Library** can be useful for appropriate background audio, but platform-specific licenses and commercial restrictions must be checked. This background is **not a substitute for demonstrating the actual featured music**.
- When official platform remix/Shorts audio features are used, review their individual permissions and creator restrictions; do not assume permission to download, cross-post or boost.
- Permission to record a guest, display their image, or reuse their musical performance should be documented separately.
- Do not publish individual contributor-journal reflections or account details without appropriate permission.

## Production ownership and sequence

- Founder retains accounts, editorial sign-off, rights records and final publishing authority.
- The project can begin with off-camera narrated video using owned sound and properly licensed images, then incorporate occasional founder-led pieces and paid/volunteer performer demonstrations with permission.
- Invite artists to collaborate *after* the project has two or three polished original pilot samples, with clear expectations about clips, credit, platforms, promotion and compensation.
- Podcast booking, co-host selection and a recording studio are **not prerequisites**.
- No default requirement to post daily; video should reward attention and prompt a visit to Earth to Ear.
