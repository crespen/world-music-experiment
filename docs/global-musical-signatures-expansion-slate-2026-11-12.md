# Earth to Ear — first global musical signature expansion (editorial slate)

**Prepared:** 2026-11-12  
**Status:** Selection targets for source verification; **not published catalog entries**.

## Purpose

Select **25 additions per continent** (150 in this first tranche) with distinct musical signatures, responsible cultural attribution, original historical context, and usable authorized listening sources. The established catalog contains 71 active recordings. This slate is an editorial research queue; titles and candidate performers **must not be assumed to be verified recordings**.

The goal is **breadth of expression, not quota-filling or national rankings**. No tradition is presumed less important because it is represented by a small sample. Categories can change when research reveals better candidates.

## Admission gate

A candidate is accepted into `music_catalog` only after finding a precise recording/performance and verifying: (1) performer or community attribution; (2) date/period, location and tradition where supportable; (3) direct, workable authorized source (public full playback preferred; clear preview labels permitted); (4) an original listener-oriented description of identifiable musical features; (5) culturally appropriate presentation and rights/ceremonial restrictions; (6) nonduplication with current catalog; (7) basic evidence citation. **Do not invent precise song titles, source URLs, performer identities or historical transmission links.** For public archive recordings the record label or uploader’s cultural designation may be dated; use accurate, respectful editorial language. Source links are not music rights.

Priority for sources: performers' official channels, Library of Congress Homegrown, Smithsonian Folkways / UNESCO Traditional Music Collection, official cultural institutions, documented local archives, community-authorized media. A catalog page is a link and cultural explanation, not an unauthorized audio host.

## Initial priorities and workflow

1. **First jazz group:** Randy Weston, John Coltrane, Thelonious Monk and Oscar Peterson (one historically well-documented performance each, ideally two contrasting performances over time in a later tranche). Search for original-source performance, not merely a YouTube search-result page. Retain full titles/dates and exact musical context.
2. **Globally underrepresented collections:** Asia, Europe, South America and Oceania currently have only 2–3 active examples. Give them early curatorial attention; do not fill the queue with one subgenre or a single urban cultural scene.
3. **African breadth:** Avoid simply adding Afrobeats or contemporary live-band performances. Field recordings, local ensembles, ritual and social genres, vocal techniques, and instrument systems should figure strongly.
4. **Responsible Indigenous coverage:** Listen only to publicly shared, appropriately attributed performances; avoid restricted ceremonial materials or falsely treating one recording as representative of all communities.
5. **Geographic convention:** Countries/communities are documented as specifically as evidence permits. Hawaiʻi is assigned here to Oceania geographically, while acknowledging it is politically part of the United States; cross-region connections can be added later.
6. **Publication:** Validate and publish in small batches of 5–10; retain original sources and cautions, test links, and allow the existing automated page generator to create pages and sitemap entries.

### Evidence and source discovery

- Smithsonian Folkways UNESCO Collection of Traditional Music: https://folkways.si.edu/unesco — 127 albums of worldwide traditional music, with field recordings from more than 70 nations. Some playback may require purchase or subscription; confirm each track's public access.
- Library of Congress Homegrown concerts: https://www.loc.gov/events/concerts-from-the-library-of-congress/folklife-concerts/ — official concert video archive, many available on LOC and YouTube.
- Library of Congress Indigenous Peoples of the Americas concerts guide: https://guides.loc.gov/indigenous-peoples-of-the-Americas-folklife/concerts
- UNESCO performing arts and intangible heritage: https://ich.unesco.org/en/performing-arts-such-as-traditional-music-dance-and-theatre-00054 — cultural-context reference, not automatic recording authorization.

## Boundary for later research

This phase documents **musical signatures**, not purported transcontinental ancestry. Similar-sounding features are observations; historical transmission, continuity, displacement, or convergence require separate evidence. Preserve early notes that may support the future African diaspora inquiry, but do not let the hypothesis predetermine catalog admissions.
