# Earth to Ear — Daily Music Discovery Standard

**Status:** Standing editorial search charter · adopted November 2026  
**Primary aim:** Find new-to-us musical experiences every day, across diverse cultures, periods, styles, and providers. Maximize genuine musical surprise and curiosity, not the appearance of complete geographic coverage. *Earth to Ear is a listening experiment, not a scholarly certification project.*

## Non-negotiable search lenses

Search across **all** of these recurring angles, never exclusively a single one:

1. **Unfamiliar musical practices.** Distinct vocal techniques, tuning, improvisation, instruments, ensemble organization, rhythms, forms, community contexts or relationships between music and movement.
2. **Familiar genres, unfamiliar perspectives.** Original contemporary rap/hip-hop, R&B/soul, country, pop, rock, dance/electronic, jazz, funk, regional popular music and beyond by artists across the world. A song can be valuable without overtly quoting an old tradition.
3. **Inherited music performed today.** Contemporary performances of longstanding traditions, including creative reinterpretation and adaptation.
4. **Musical innovation in its own time.** Older recordings that were once new: early jazz, blues, highlife, Afrobeat, regional popular forms, early electronic music, etc. An older recording is not automatically "traditional."
5. **Hybrids, crossings and unexpected pairings.** Credible examples of independent innovation and musical exchange; don't assume sonic similarity proves historical influence.
6. **Different listening contexts.** Informal and community recordings, solo work, field recordings released for public listening, live performances, independent releases, and studio work—not just professionally filmed concerts.

## Geographic and source coverage

Search in **currently open collections (Africa, North America)** and **not-yet-open collections (Asia, Europe, South America, Oceania)** every week. Do not use political borders as a musical quota; attend to communities, languages, diasporas, and internal diversity. "Locked" is a website status, not a reason to exclude discovery leads.

Actively rotate among **artist and label channels, local/regional broadcasters, independent radio, public archives and libraries, community and Indigenous-led organizations, cultural institutions, festival recordings, public broadcaster sessions, and major video/music platforms**. Seek creators' own uploads and regional sources. Prevent NPR Tiny Desk, KEXP, or Smithsonian from becoming the default: aim for **at most one Tiny Desk/KEXP item per daily brief**, unless unusually valuable, and routinely seek at least one previously unused provider.

## Two independent time axes

Every candidate should be described on both axes when ascertainable:
- **Recording/performance:** historical/archival, recent/contemporary, or unknown.
- **Musical approach:** inherited tradition, historic innovation, contemporary original popular/experimental music, reinterpretation, hybrid, or uncertain.

Do not confuse the time the sound was captured with the age of a composition, genre, or cultural practice. Do not claim "old song" merely because a contemporary performer has traditional influences.

## Daily search and editorial decisions

1. Begin by checking Earth to Ear's current catalog at https://earthtoear.com/explore/ (and when accessible the connected `crespen/world-music-experiment` catalog and this project's discovery ledger) for duplicates by recording, artist/performance, and listening URL. Avoid repeating recent recommendations unless there is a distinct performance or musical reason.
2. Sample varied search queries: one for a striking musical technique or practice, one for a new contemporary artist in a familiar genre from a different locale, one for a historic recording/reinterpretation, one for a community/independent source, and one wild-card inquiry. Change traditions, countries, regions, genres and platforms across days.
3. Look for approximately **4–6 good candidates per daily brief**. Strong discoveries matter more than hitting a number; if only two good finds appear, report two. Prefer diversity of musical **sound and creative method**, not merely different nationality labels.
4. Basic usability check only: reasonably correct title and performer, a direct publicly shareable source that appears to offer enough free audio to hear the performance (ideally 30 seconds or longer), and a distinctive one-sentence reason to listen. Avoid paywall-only leads. Note source restrictions if obvious; don't demand exhaustive provenance or permanent regional-playback guarantees.
5. Respect publicly stated restrictions for culturally sensitive/community/ceremonial music. Prefer performances artists or communities intentionally make public. Do not embed, download or republish source audio as part of discovery.
6. Identify standout finds and **which listening dimension is new** (e.g. voice technique, rhythm, ensemble, new popular sound, different context, historical contrast). If evidence is only descriptive, label it a *reason to sample*, not something you personally heard.
7. Propose status **"Add candidate"**, **"Investigate further"**, or **"Not worth adding"**. Recommendations do not alter the live catalog or journal registry. Founder approves additions separately.
8. Maintain a dated list of unique promising candidates in `docs/music-discovery-ledger.md` when GitHub access is available. If not, include a compact ledger-ready table in the brief. Do not claim a ledger write that wasn't performed.
9. Every week, re-examine source/format/style coverage and choose a new search direction; don't permanently prefer a particular provider or a western-harmony yardstick.

## Daily brief format

Title: **Earth to Ear · Daily Listening Discoveries · [date]**  
Offer a concise, scannable table with 4–6 candidates if available, each with:
- **Artist and exact recording/performance title** with direct source listening link.
- **Cultural and/or geographical context** (avoid declaring one recording representative of a people).
- **What makes this different** (a specific listening invitation rather than empty adjectives).
- **Time axes**: recording era; inherited/new/modern reinterpretation as applicable.
- **Source provider** and **editorial status** (Add candidate / Investigate further).

Close with **"Most surprising lead"** and **"Source we hadn't used before"** when applicable, plus one suggested cross-genre or cross-era pairing.

Never present a candidate as already in the live catalog; never auto-publish. Avoid exoticizing language, universal claims about a culture, overconfident claims of having listened, and elaborate auditing rituals. The music is the priority.
