# NORTH AMERICAN MUSICAL ATLAS

**Earth to Ear | A Global Listening Experiment**

**Volume I · Edition v0.3 · November 13, 2026**  
**Status:** Internal working inventory; country-first and typed edition. **Method:** [Global Classification Standard v1.0](../atlas-classification-standard-v1.md). Companion volumes: [Africa v0.3](african-musical-atlas-v0-3.md), [South America v0.2](south-american-musical-atlas-v0-2.md).  
**Basis:** North America v0.1 and *Musical Atlas Review: Africa and North America v0.1* (November 12, 2026).

This edition creates one country/region-centered view instead of placing the United States in seven densely subdivided sections while assigning other places only a few entries. **v0.1 remains available as a preserved detailed research backlog**, not as a balanced set of public genre tags. These lists are candidate labels, not fully verified genres or recording metadata.

## Editorial approach and v0.3 classification

**Rule:** Each candidate line is typed [G], [F], [P], [S], [A] or [R] and placed once in a primary historical lens based on the entity’s formative transformation, not ongoing performance. All three categories can be practiced today. See [global standard](../atlas-classification-standard-v1.md).

- **Three working listening-era categories.** Living and inherited traditions; historical transformations; contemporary expressions. These describe research contexts, not a linear ladder: a community practice can be alive, historically rooted, and contemporary simultaneously.
- **Classification is typed.** Genre, scene, musical form, instrument, technique, sacred/ceremonial practice, artist and cross-border link are different entities. A UNESCO cultural inscription does not make an urban popular genre a village tradition.
- **Equal curiosity, not equal numerical quotas.** Country sections receive comparable research attention; we do not expand one nation's micro-genres while reducing others to a single generic label.
- **Sacred and composed music belong everywhere.** Include art/concert and religious music with the same care as popular or community music, subject to community permissions.
- **Music-first catalog rule.** A reference film is NOT the default recording. Prefer exact authorized, substantially uninterrupted musical performances. Documentary-first entries need separate musical-segment verification and must be labeled as research context until suitable listening is confirmed.
- **Evidence and caution.** This is an editorial reclassification of candidate names plus reviewer-identified research leads; individual entries do not become source-verified by being included in the atlas.

## Geographic and cross-border guide

- The United States (including Alaska), Canada, Mexico, Greenland, seven Central American countries, and the Caribbean islands and territories are included. Hawaii is geographically cross-linked with Oceania while retaining its U.S. political location.
- The territory and nation headings here are browsing aids, not exclusive claims about origins or ownership. Traditions cross borders, oceans, languages and diasporas.
- Three categories are practical research lenses, not strict historical bins. Genres can appear across periods and circulate through several places.
- Acknowledged shared developments include **punk, heavy metal, synth-pop, shoegaze** and other transatlantic styles. Never tag an exclusive North American origin based on a scene's importance alone.

## Cross-border and historical relationship register

- **Jazz, blues, spirituals, gospel and work song:** relationships must be documented rather than presented as a single inevitable lineage; many genres developed in overlapping eras and communities.
- **Salsa, son, rumba, mambo, Latin jazz and boogaloo:** Cuban, Puerto Rican and New York contributions and Afro-Caribbean circulation; country-of-record is not necessarily country-of-origin.
- **Garifuna networks:** Caribbean origins and mainland Central American migration; songs can belong to cross-border communities.
- **Reggae and dub:** Jamaican centers with later circulation through North American, Caribbean and African communities; distinguish original development from later regional scenes.
- **Punk, heavy metal, shoegaze and synth-pop:** British/European and North American interchanges; no single-country claim without evidence.
- **Sacred and concert music:** shape-note and Sacred Harp, Black church gospel, Southern gospel, Christian popular music, Native American and Inuit community music, classical and experimental composition all belong in continental research.

## New listening-source gate

**Default:** visitors should arrive at a real musical performance, not introductory narration. Prefer a complete song or coherent full performance at a source controlled by its creator or authorized presenter. Documentaries belong in the research references unless a specific substantial musical segment can be located and independently tested. Record the source, exact performance, start offset, extent of uninterrupted music, narration burden and playback test status before adding the selection.
