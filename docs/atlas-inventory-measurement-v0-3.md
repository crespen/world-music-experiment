# Musical Atlas inventory measurement — harmonized research editions

**Date:** November 13, 2026  
**Coverage:** [Africa v0.3](atlases/african-musical-atlas-v0-3.md), [North America v0.3](atlases/north-american-musical-atlas-v0-3.md), [South America v0.2](atlases/south-american-musical-atlas-v0-2.md).  
**Method:** [Global Atlas classification standard v1.0](atlas-classification-standard-v1.md).

The figures count **typed candidate bullets in principal geographic sections**, not verified distinct genres, recordings, musical communities or performances. Some bullets contain related variants and will require canonical IDs or finer splitting. Geography headings are navigational units, not mutually exclusive cultural territories.

Type key: **G** genre/style candidate; **F** song or poetic form; **P** performance/ritual/ensemble practice; **S** scene, movement or umbrella; **A** composed/concert music; **R** unresolved research lead.

**Do not report 1,411 genres, or even 696 verified genres.** All counts are provisional editorial classifications, and country-wide source anchors do not constitute entry-level provenance. Africa and North America lost placeholder and duplicate bullets while some candidate histories were consolidated or added. These figures are not directly comparable to earlier v0.1 raw-label totals.

## Edition-level changes

- **Africa:** Ghanaian highlife was consolidated into a canonical family and historically related variants; mahraganat and Kenyan taarab/chakacha corrected; Tunisia's La Rachidia-era revival added; country-level art-music research slots standardized without treating unsourced placeholders as verified works.
- **North America:** Caribbean categories were re-examined by formation period rather than current activity, including Jamaica, Cuba, Haiti, Puerto Rico, Trinidad and Tobago, and the eastern Caribbean. Calypso/kaiso and steelband/steelpan relationships were consolidated; shared-country/era references were retained without duplicating canonical labels.
- **South America:** Brazilian improvised forms, rural music, immigrant community music and rock; San Basilio de Palenque; Peruvian scissors dance and rock; Venezuelan Corpus Christi and eastern forms; Oruro diablada; Rapa Nui's Polynesian connection; and new cross-volume source references were added.

## Open validation obligations

1. Verify exact type and formative chronology against item-level primary/community or specialist sources. The first pass is not definitive even where the metadata format is complete.
2. Deduplicate canonical IDs and aliases across geographic volumes and separate parent genres from variants, performance practices and scenes.
3. Build a bidirectional evidence register for transregional circulation and documented influence; similarities alone do not establish contact.
4. Keep source recordings and educational documentaries separate from the Atlas taxonomy and subject to the music-first publishing gate.
5. Before any public Atlas launch, audit permissions, respectful labels, active validity and narrative balance with knowledgeable contributors.

**No public Atlas pages or Listening Library records were deployed as part of this research update.**
