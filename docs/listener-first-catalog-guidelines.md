# Earth to Ear — Listener-first catalog guidelines

Updated 10 November 2026.

## Purpose

Earth to Ear invites curious people to **encounter, listen to, and reflect on music from diverse communities**. It is not designed to be a comprehensive ethnomusicology archive or an instrument that claims authoritative findings about musical traditions. Personal journal entries are reflections, not scholarly verdicts.

## Add music without unnecessary gatekeeping

A useful new entry needs:
1. A reasonably clear title and performer/group name (or "Performers not identified" when unknown).
2. Country/region and a helpful, **provisional** tradition or style label that doesn't pretend to represent an entire culture.
3. A direct link to a publicly shared recording with enough free audio to make listening worthwhile. The target is 30 seconds or more; full performances are especially welcome.
4. A short question or description that stimulates curiosity rather than testing musical expertise.

A link to the original public source is enough: **do not download or republish the music** without separate rights. No promise of permanent regional playback availability is necessary. Broken links are routine maintenance, not a reason to withhold a substantial, diverse catalog.

## Listener-facing labels

Use clear descriptions like:
- "Watch performance" / "Video · YouTube"
- "Listen to audio" / "Audio · Wikimedia Commons"
- "Hear recording or preview" / "Track / preview · Smithsonian Folkways"
- "Listen on Bandcamp" / "Track · Bandcamp"
- "Listen at Library of Congress" / "Historical audio · Library of Congress"

Do **not** expose internal notes about HTTP checks, territorial availability, provisional licensing or exact excerpt verification on listening cards. Those belong in private link-health tooling. Do not claim a full recording is freely available when the source explicitly offers only a preview.

Keep **performer credit separate from uploader, host or field-recordist credit**. If the performer isn't known, say so without guessing.

## Diversity is the priority

Seek variation across **communities, language, region, instrumentation, historical and contemporary practice, vocal traditions, rhythms, and the relationships between music and everyday life**. Do not use a one-per-country quota or rank cultures by musical value. A single performance doesn't represent a whole country, ethnicity, language or tradition.

Keep Africa, North America and future continent collections distinct for browsing, while allowing comparisons across geographic boundaries. Include Indigenous music that artists and communities have *chosen to share publicly*, without placing private or restricted ceremonial material in the library.

### Initial breadth expansion · November 2026

Added ten source-linked, journal-enabled performances: Pamyua, Jeremy Dutcher, The Halluci Nation, Lila Downs, Cimafunk, Lakou Mizik, iLe, Rhiannon Giddens, Alsarah & The Nubatones, and Emel. The active library contains 42 recordings, with 31 from Africa and 11 from North America.

Indigenous Ohkay Owingeh music remains an interest for future community-led sourcing; **no private ceremonial recording has been published**.

## Homepage editorial schedule

Approved music enters the library immediately. The founder-controlled "What's New" lineup is separate and protects each featured item for at least 30 days. On the 1st and 15th the rotation may add one or two queued selections when room is available. Prefer contrasting listening experiences over the newest titles alone.

The November 2026 queue gives priority to Pamyua (Yup’ik drumsongs, Alaska) and Alsarah & The Nubatones (Sudanese diaspora / East African pop), followed by previously queued recordings. Founder controls can change these choices.

## Keep improving

Add new recordings regularly, invite suggestions and corrections, and repair nonworking listening links as they arise. Use thoughtful musical curiosity—not an endless certification process—to guide growth.
